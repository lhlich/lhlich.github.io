The most consequential modeling choice is often the meaning of a training example. Write down what was observed, what the model predicts, and the distribution under which that prediction will be used.

For example, an unclicked displayed document is an observed zero for click. It is not automatically an observed zero for relevance. A never-displayed document has no click outcome from that session. These distinctions determine which examples may be labeled, weighted, or excluded.

Define a row explicitly: (query, document snapshot, user/context at request time, exposure, outcome within a specified window). Deduplicate before splitting where appropriate; split by time and relevant grouping units. Fit preprocessing on training data. Normalization does not remove future information or duplicates: it can itself leak if fitted on the whole dataset. This uses the derivative of log-sum-exp. Cross-entropy against empirical hard labels corresponds to categorical maximum likelihood. Fixed soft targets also define a cross-entropy objective; they need not be observations of ground truth. L2-regularized likelihood can be viewed as MAP with an appropriate Gaussian prior, but supplying teacher targets is not by itself a Bayesian prior. [Softmax and likelihood reference]

Numerical example: logits [2,1,0] produce probabilities approximately [0.6652,0.2447,0.0900]. If class 0 is correct, loss≈0.4076 and gradient≈[−0.3348,0.2447,0.0900]. The components sum to zero because shifting every logit by the same constant leaves probabilities unchanged.

3. Contrastive learning is a task construction; InfoNCE uses cross-entropy

Encode one query and a candidate set containing its positive document plus negatives. Define candidate logits s(q,dj)/τ. The label is the positive candidate's index. The resulting objective is ordinary categorical cross-entropy over that candidate set:

@@EQ:contrastive@@

The gradient on a negative score is pj/τ; on the positive it is (p+−1)/τ. Confusing negatives receive larger repulsive updates. Temperature changes both probability sharpness and the gradient scale; it is not merely a display-time rescaling.

InfoNCE is a contrastive objective with this form; the CPC paper develops a density-ratio interpretation under its sampling setup. That interpretation relies on how positives and negatives are drawn. “Contrastive loss” also includes objectives such as margin-based triplet losses, so not every contrastive objective is softmax cross-entropy. [CPC / InfoNCE]

Why the negative sampler changes the task

Suppose q is “jaguar charging issue.” Random animal documents are easy negatives; nearby automobile support articles force the model to distinguish vehicle relevance and intent. But a second valid troubleshooting article may be a false negative. Hard-negative mining should improve discrimination without routinely penalizing valid alternatives.

For scores [2,1,0] and τ=1, the positive loss is 0.4076. At τ=0.5 the logits become [4,2,0], loss≈0.1429. This particular example becomes easier because the positive already leads. If a negative leads, reducing τ increases the penalty. Smaller temperature is not uniformly better.

With a batch of B matched query-document pairs, Q and D have shape B×d and S=QDᵀ/τ has shape B×B. The diagonal supplies positive indices and the other columns act as negatives. This assumes off-diagonal pairs are usable negatives. Duplicate documents and multiple relevant documents violate the simplest interpretation. Use masking, a multi-positive objective, or better supervision as appropriate. Sampling corrections are needed if the intended objective is an approximation to a full-corpus softmax; an uncorrected sampled objective need not estimate that same distribution.

Distillation connection: minimize CE(pteacher,pstudent) or KL(pteacher‖pstudent); for a fixed teacher, they differ by the teacher entropy, which is constant in student parameters. At temperature T, derivatives with respect to student raw logits carry 1/T; conventional T² scaling compensates for approximate gradient shrinkage at high T. It is a training convention, not a universal mathematical requirement. [Distillation paper]

ListwiseCross-entropy between target gains normalized over a list and softmax scoresModels competition within a set; depends on list construction and still need not optimize the actual downstream utility

With exactly one positive and one negative, the contrastive loss reduces to log(1+exp((s−−s+)/τ)). Thus a familiar pairwise logistic objective is a two-candidate softmax loss. This algebra is useful because it connects an implementation to its assumptions.

“Listwise” alone does not ensure diversity or coverage. If the utility rewards complementary evidence, the training target or objective must encode it. Metric-weighted pairwise updates, such as weighting swaps by their effect on NDCG, offer another route. Model family and loss family are separate: a tree ranker is not synonymous with one particular target or evaluation metric.

5. Metrics: declare the unit, labels, and denominator

Recall@k needs a relevant-document set. With incomplete judgments, report recall over known positives and judging coverage; do not label it true corpus recall. MRR uses the reciprocal rank of the first relevant result, so later useful results do not improve that query's score. NDCG supports graded relevance and position discounting:

@@EQ:ndcg@@

IDCG is the DCG of an ideal ordering under the declared judging universe and cutoff. Declare what happens when IDCG=0; exclusion and zero are different aggregation conventions. Average per query or per user according to the evaluation goal rather than accidentally weighting by the number of documents. [IR evaluation reference]

Worked example: retrieved grades [0,2,1,0,3] use gains [0,3,1,0,7]. DCG@5=3/log₂3+1/log₂4+7/log₂6≈5.100. Ideal grades [3,2,1,0,0] give IDCG≈9.393, so NDCG≈0.543. With any positive grade relevant, reciprocal rank=1/2. If one of the zeros is actually unjudged, this calculation is conditional on an explicit zero-imputation convention; it does not establish that document's irrelevance.

AUC is the probability that a randomly selected positive scores higher than a randomly selected negative, with half credit for ties. It evaluates pair ordering rather than probability calibration. Pooling examples across unrelated queries can reward between-query offsets, so inspect the evaluation population. For rare positives, also examine precision-recall behavior and operating points. A monotonic score transformation can preserve ranking and AUC while making probability calibration much worse.

In RAG or agent retrieval, success may require several complementary sources. Add evidence coverage, duplicate rate, and downstream task success measured with a fixed generator and budget. Ranking position can still matter because of truncation and the consumer's attention behavior. Switching from human search to agent consumption does not automatically make order irrelevant.

6. Position bias and counterfactual claims

A simple examination model writes click=examination×relevance, with the necessary conditional-independence assumptions. Examination propensities can support inverse-propensity weighting of observed feedback. This is not equivalent to weighting every unclicked result as irrelevant, nor does a position-only propensity fix trust bias, selection bias, or all interactions. Estimated propensities and very large weights create additional error and variance. [Unbiased learning to rank]

For a logged action policy μ and target policy π, a basic off-policy estimator is:

@@EQ:ips@@

It needs known or reliably estimated logging probabilities, support (μ(a|x)>0 wherever π may act), consistent rewards, and the appropriate action unit. A slate is not generally interchangeable with independently selected items. This policy ratio is conceptually distinct from correcting the probability of examining a displayed result.

If a binary action is logged with μ=0.25 and the target selects it with π=0.5, its weight is 2. A reward of 1 contributes 2/n to the estimate. If μ=0, no finite weight can recover the missing outcome. Weight clipping can reduce variance but generally introduces bias.

Scoring a candidate model on a long-term holdout is useful predictive evaluation, but it does not reveal outcomes caused by the candidate's unexecuted decisions. A randomized online comparison can estimate the effect of actually serving different policies under its experimental design. Repeatedly tuning against a holdout also makes it less independent, even if the holdout's serving policy never changes.

Solutions and reasoning
  1. Divide the denominator by exp(s+/τ). This gives log(1+exp((s−−s+)/τ)); gradients are (p+−1)/τ and p−/τ.
  2. Position shortcuts: evaluate with randomized exposure or position-controlled judgments. Click-attractive irrelevant results: inspect blinded relevance judgments versus click labels. Distribution or leakage: use temporal, grouped splits and query-slice results. These are hypotheses, not conclusions from the metric alone.
  3. AUC ignores probability accuracy. A cost threshold depends on calibrated probabilities and deployment prevalence, not only positive-negative ordering. Recalibrate on representative data and evaluate utility at the actual operating point.
