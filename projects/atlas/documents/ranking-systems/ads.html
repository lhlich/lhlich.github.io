03 · Ads / Systems study

Rank ads by expected value

An ad score represents an estimate with economic units. Before choosing a model, identify what event the advertiser pays for and which constraints make a candidate eligible.

1. Name the event and the bid unit

Assume the service receives an allocated ad slot, user context, and eligible campaign candidates. It produces value estimates for an allocation service. Targeting, remaining budget, frequency caps, creative policy, and pacing decisions must be reflected in eligibility. Allocation and payment rules are a separate specified contract.

Do not assume every ad auction is second-price or that the ranking score is the charge. For example, Google’s actual CPC documentation describes additional thresholds and quality factors. The formulas here are simplified expected values for a teaching scenario, not a billing algorithm.

CPC bid (currency / click): expected currency / 1000 impressions = 1000 · P(click) · bid_CPC CPA bid (currency / post-click conversion): expected currency / 1000 impressions = 1000 · P(click) · P(conversion | click) · bid_CPA

The CPA expression assumes the conversion is attributed through a click. View-through conversions or other objectives require a different model. The factor of 1,000 gives per-mille units. Multiplying a CPC bid by CVR would mix a conversion objective into a click-priced value without a stated economic reason.

Check the units

These estimates exclude pricing rules, fees, budget optimization, and auction incentives.

2. Preserve eligibility through the scoring pipeline

A cheap pre-scorer should preserve candidates likely to win under the expensive model, not simply maximize its own AUC. Evaluate shortlist recall against the final objective on replayable eligible sets. Sample expensive scores for dropped candidates to inspect the blind spot, with appropriate cost controls.

A logistic or tree baseline establishes feature and label quality. Neural models with learned categorical embeddings, crosses, or task-specific heads are candidates for later evaluation. The DCN-v2 paper offers one interaction architecture; it does not imply that every ads workload needs it.

Protect final allocation from stale state. A cached prediction can be reused only with an explicit age and compatibility rule; budget and policy eligibility may need another check. If required state is unavailable, returning no ad can be safer for correctness than serving an ineligible “popular” fallback.

3. Conversion labels have both delay and selection

Clicks arrive quickly; conversions may arrive after the training snapshot. Define an attribution event, deduplication key, time window, and revision policy. A recent impression with no recorded conversion is not the same as a mature negative.

A simple baseline trains on mature cohorts and accepts staleness. Alternatives model the observation delay or use corrections under explicit assumptions; they require evaluation across cohort age. Chapelle’s delayed-feedback paper formalizes this problem. A single universal waiting period is not justified across products.

Post-click CVR training also selects users who clicked. Combining CTR and CVR estimates requires attention to that selection and consistency between the heads. ESMM is an example of modeling the impression → click → conversion sequence jointly. It is not a general cure for unmeasured confounding or every attribution issue.

4. Calibrate to the serving population

For a predicted probability near 0.1, inspect how often the event occurs in comparable held-out examples. Reliability diagrams, log loss, and business-relevant slices reveal different failures; aggregate calibration can hide a segment error. If negative sampling changes the training class balance, account for it before interpreting a raw model output as a serving probability.

Fit calibration on held-out, representative data, with mature labels for the conversion task. Sigmoid calibration has a constrained shape; isotonic is more flexible but can overfit with little data and introduce ties. Neither is categorically superior. The scikit-learn guide explains the trade-off. Version the calibrator with the model and monitor drift after launch.

Calibration matters because two candidates can have different bids. A probability distortion can change the ordering of probability × bid even when the probability-only ordering is preserved. Good calibration still does not prove the economic objective or auction mechanism is correct.

5. Evaluate value, user impact, and feedback

Track shortlist survival, mature outcome prediction, calibration by cohort, latency, budget violations, and negative feedback. Revenue alone can improve while advertiser outcomes deteriorate; inspect conversions and advertiser-level concentration as well. Pacing and auction changes can create interference across impressions and campaigns, which should inform the experimental design.

Exploration needs a defined allocation policy, budget constraints, and logged selection probabilities. Do not substitute empirical click rate at a position for an examination propensity. For position-bias corrections, specify the observation model and validate the estimator; the counterfactual ranking literature makes those assumptions explicit.

Work the example

Ad A has CTR 0.04, conditional CVR 0.10, and a CPA bid of 30. Ad B has CTR 0.06, conditional CVR 0.02, and a CPA bid of 50. Which has higher simplified expected value?

Answer and the next question

A: 1,000 × 0.04 × 0.10 × 30 = 120 per mille. B: 1,000 × 0.06 × 0.02 × 50 = 60. A wins under this simplified value model. Before serving it, verify current eligibility, probability calibration, the attribution definition, and the actual allocation mechanism.
