BM25, beyond TF-IDF

A compact guide to term-frequency saturation, document-length normalization and inverse document frequency.

What changes?

Unlike raw term counts, BM25 assumes that repeating a matching query term helps but with diminishing returns. A passage mentioning a term five times is not necessarily five times as relevant as a passage mentioning it once.

score(q,d) = Σ IDF(t) · [f(t,d)(k₁ + 1)] / [f(t,d) + k₁(1 − b + b·|d|/avgdl)]

Interpretation

k₁ controls term-frequency saturation. b controls how strongly document length is normalized. The IDF term rewards relatively rare query terms.

A shorter document is not always better: length normalization adjusts the expected amount of matching evidence, rather than introducing a universal short-document bonus.

Where it fits

BM25 is a strong lexical retrieval baseline and is complementary to semantic retrieval. Hybrid search can combine lexical candidates with dense retrieval results before reranking.
