Primary references and optional reading

The guide's calculations, code, and design exercises are original. The references below establish the underlying methods. Read the indicated topic rather than every paper end to end. Sources were checked while compiling these notes on October 3, 2026 PT; no vendor benchmark or current model leaderboard is assumed.

  1. Manning, Raghavan, and Schütze — Introduction to Information Retrieval. Consult inverted indexes, BM25, and evaluation chapters.
  2. Broder et al. — Efficient Query Evaluation Using a Two-Level Retrieval Process. WAND's bound-based retrieval idea.
  3. Vespa — WAND: Accelerated OR Search. Distinguish algorithm intuition from deployed operator guarantees.
  4. Faiss — Index reference. Flat, IVF, HNSW, and compressed vector representations.
  5. Stanford CS231n — Linear classification. Softmax, cross-entropy, likelihood, and regularization.
  6. van den Oord, Li, and Vinyals — Representation Learning with Contrastive Predictive Coding. InfoNCE and its sampling interpretation.
  7. Joachims, Swaminathan, and Schnabel — Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback. Examination bias and propensity-weighted learning.
  8. Vaswani et al. — Attention Is All You Need. Scaled dot-product attention and multi-head shapes.
  9. Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean — Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network. Soft targets and temperature.
  10. Ioffe and Szegedy — Batch Normalization; Ba, Kiros, and Hinton — Layer Normalization. Compare normalization axes and train/eval behavior.
  11. Loshchilov and Hutter — Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization. Why adaptive-optimizer L2 and decoupled decay differ.
