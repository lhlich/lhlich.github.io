Two search systems share an index but serve different consumers: a person choosing a result while typing, and a model assembling evidence for an answer. Their budgets, ranking objectives, and failure modes should differ.

Core reading. Reconstruct both designs, including their update and learning paths. For each decision, identify the quality benefit, the memory/latency/energy cost, and the data required to evaluate it.

1. Scope, assumptions, and public platform examples

On-device search is a useful design scenario for studying retrieval under privacy, resource, and freshness constraints. Assume offline operation, permissioned personal content, a changing corpus, and constrained memory/energy. The numbers below are teaching inputs that should be replaced with measurements on a specified device.

Apple's public Core Spotlight material establishes a private on-device index, semantic queries, item engagement signals, and reindexing support. Its WWDC26 session describes a SpotlightSearchTool, full-item hydration through an index delegate, scoped guidance profiles, and computation stages. These are useful examples of interfaces and constraints; they do not establish the internal implementation of any particular product. [Core Spotlight] [Search tool]

2. Design A: interactive, as-you-type personal search

Example: a user types “pho,” then “phot,” looking for Photos or a recent photo-related note. Assume 100,000 indexed text-bearing items across authorized sources, plus a small inventory of apps/settings. The first useful results should appear quickly; a slower semantic refinement can arrive later. Define a responsiveness target on the actual device rather than assuming an encoder and scan finish in a fixed number of milliseconds.

Concurrency matters: results for “pho” can finish after results for “phot.” Tag each request, cancel obsolete work when possible, and prevent stale results from overwriting the current UI. Debouncing reduces repeated expensive computation but adds waiting time. Test fast typing, pauses, backspaces, and query replacement rather than only complete strings.

Incremental filtering needs a completeness argument

Suppose the top two completions for “pho” are phone and photo; photon ranks third. Filtering those two for “phot” returns only photo, although photon belongs in the new top two. Reuse a complete prefix range, requery the prefix structure, or refill when cached coverage is insufficient. Even a complete matching set may require rescoring as query-dependent features change.

Ranking signals: title/app exact match, last-token prefix match, field-aware lexical score, fuzzy distance, semantic score where available, recency, decayed usage, and query-prefix affinity. A missing semantic score should be represented as “channel did not run,” not automatically zero similarity. Usage affinity can be valuable for navigation, but its strength must be established by ablation and it can create feedback lock-in.

Frecency with half-life H gives a use at age Δt weight 2−Δt/H. Store a score and its timestamp; before adding a use, decay the stored score to the new time and add one. This costs O(1) per update without retaining every event. The frecency drill makes the time invariant explicit.

Cross-source ranking and section allocation

App-launch, mail, file, and contact scores may have incompatible scales and outcome definitions. First choose a common utility and comparable exposure context. Calibration can map scores to an outcome rate under the data distribution used for calibration; it cannot turn biased placement logs into P(engagement if shown at the top) without suitable exposure data or assumptions. Randomized/interleaved exposure can help identify placement effects.

A baseline can use a global ranker with source indicators and query features, plus eligibility, deduplication, and section caps. Alternatively use source rankers and validated calibration. Top Hit thresholds trade coverage against error cost. Evaluate the page as a whole: moving a section changes both its visibility and competitors' exposure. Separate known-item success from exploratory engagement.

3. Design B: retrieval for an on-device assistant

Example: “Which hikes did I do with Alex last month, and how far did I go?” The system needs correct identity resolution, date boundaries, item coverage, and arithmetic. A list of individually relevant snippets may still be insufficient to compute the total.

  1. Determine whether retrieval is needed. Personal-data questions need authorized sources; general knowledge may not. Ambiguous requests can need clarification.
  2. Generate and validate a structured request. Represent text, date range, people IDs, item types, aggregation, and limits. Resolve timezone/date arithmetic in deterministic code; a name such as Alex still requires disambiguation.
  3. Retrieve eligible evidence. Apply hard metadata/permission constraints, then lexical and semantic retrieval as appropriate. Preserve the query and filter provenance.
  4. Compute over the correct population. Exact count/sum/group operations should use the complete eligible match set, not just the top-k text hits. Return completeness/partial-result status with aggregates.
  5. Select a compact evidence set. Rerank, remove redundant passages, retain complementary evidence, hydrate full items only when needed, and measure serialized tokens.
  6. Answer with references or uncertainty. Validate cited IDs, numeric consistency, and whether the evidence supports the requested conclusion. Bound tool use by remaining latency, tokens, and expected benefit.
Empty results are not proof of absence. An index may be stale or a soft text match too narrow. Diagnose first. Do not silently widen “last month” or replace the requested person. A broader diagnostic query must remain distinguishable from evidence answering the original constraints. Never relax authorization.

The context budget is a resource contract

Read the runtime model's context capacity. Apple's public material includes different values across versions/devices; 4,096 is a valid worked budget, not a universal current constant. A WWDC26 example shows contextSize values of 4,096 on 26.0 and 8,192 on newer 27.0 devices. [Version-dependent context size]

Count the actual serialized representation with the target tokenizer. IDs, separators, tool results, and conversation history consume context. The packing drill requires a counter and recounts the complete candidate evidence string. Its evidence budget is what remains after other reservations. Recount after subsequent tool calls; an initial budget does not cover an unbounded trajectory.

Candidate recall is still an upstream ceiling. The final pack needs both high precision and coverage of the subquestions. MMR can suppress near-duplicates using relevance minus a redundancy penalty, but embedding novelty is only a proxy for evidence complementarity. A distinct passage can be irrelevant; two similar passages can contain different required facts. Tune against downstream evidence sufficiency.

Teach and evaluate the behavior separately from retrieval

Create synthetic corpora and questions with executable reference queries, expected IDs, and expected aggregates; include ambiguous people, missing evidence, and questions that need no tools. Verify generated training trajectories by execution and audit whether the expected answer follows. Distillation or adapter tuning can train query generation and grounded responses. Synthetic correctness does not establish coverage of real usage.

Use an oracle-evidence intervention: rerun with verified relevant evidence and a fixed consumer. Failure despite sufficient evidence implicates the generation/formatting path; improvement implicates upstream evidence construction. It is a diagnostic intervention, not a guarantee that only one component is faulty. Treat retrieved text as untrusted data and validate tool arguments and permissions outside the model.

4. Learning when raw personal logs remain on device

Local history can directly update recency, frecency, and affinity without a global training loop. A global model may start from public/synthetic/judged examples and receive privacy-preserving federated updates if the product's policy permits. Raw-data locality alone is not a formal privacy guarantee: model updates can leak information.

In a simple user-level DP construction, each participant's entire contribution is clipped to norm C. For an unweighted sum under add/remove adjacency, its L2 sensitivity is bounded by C; replacement adjacency can require 2C. Add appropriately calibrated Gaussian noise and use an accountant for participant sampling and composition over rounds. Secure aggregation controls access to individual updates; DP limits what released aggregates/models reveal. Weighted aggregation needs a corresponding sensitivity analysis.

For a fixed cohort size m and fixed noise multiplier, noise added to the sum is divided by m in the average. That does not establish a universal 1/m privacy–utility improvement: sampling rate, total population, rounds, clipping bias, participation bias, and the target privacy budget also matter. Central DP, local DP, and distributed-noise designs have different trust assumptions.

Apple has published an app-selection PFL case study and work on federated evaluation/personalization. Use these as evidence that the approach is practical, not evidence that a particular search product uses the same protocol. [PFL case study] [Federated evaluation]

Preserve exposure semantics locally: shown does not imply examined, and unclicked does not imply irrelevant. Controlled exploration can help estimate examination or placement effects, subject to UX/privacy constraints. Synthetic regressions, representative consented studies, federated metrics, and online experiments answer different questions; none is a universal substitute for the others.

5. Updates, permissions, and graceful degradation

Use mutable delta segments plus background compaction where appropriate. Tombstones suppress deleted content before physical reclamation. Recheck authorization against authoritative state before returning sensitive content; if that check is unavailable, protected results must fail closed. Index-time filters improve efficiency but do not establish freshness of permissions.

Version the analyzer, document encoder, query encoder, model, and index. For an embedding upgrade, retain compatible query/index pairs and use a planned dual-index transition or another explicitly compatible migration. Lazy background re-embedding needs coverage tracking and disk headroom. Rollback must restore a compatible pair, not just revert a model file.

Under memory pressure or a missing downloaded model, retain a useful lexical path. Chunked scans cap work memory; reranker candidate caps bound compute; deadlines and cancellation prevent obsolete keystrokes wasting energy. Cache keys must include relevant identity, query, model/index versions, and invalidation state. A cloud fallback is a separate architecture with an explicit data contract, not an assumed exception to offline/privacy requirements.

Drills

  1. Design a two-stage response for “pho” → “phot,” including cancellation and semantic refinement. Explain why a cached top-k alone is insufficient.
  2. Answer the hiking question with 2,000 evidence tokens. Identify which operations need complete match coverage and which can use top-k.
  3. A federated model improves aggregate engagement but hurts new users. Propose measurements that separate personalization lock-in, cohort bias, and actual modeling error.
Solutions and reasoning
  1. Run fresh prefix retrieval or reuse a complete index range; tag results by query generation and ignore obsolete completions. Debounce/gate the costly channel, then rerank only results for the current query.
  2. Identity/date resolution and total distance require the right complete eligible set, deduplicated with consistent units. Top-k can choose illustrative details for the narrative. The aggregate should carry coverage/completeness metadata; packing cannot repair incomplete aggregation.
  3. Compare cold-start slices and exposure-controlled evaluations, inspect which devices participate, and ablate affinity features. Evaluate both personalized and global-only predictions on representative cohorts before assigning cause.
